## Context

In the OptiPEX project, the goal is to optimize passenger experience onboard public
transport systems and improve pedestrian safety in shared spaces. Being the technical lead of
this project, I develop methods to communicate with and localize pedestrians
using UWB technology, which is then fused with AI/ML-based pedestrian detection.
I also manage the development and deployment of AI models that detect the
occupancy level of individual compartments, enabling smarter public transport.

## Locating pedestrians with ultra-wideband

A vehicle in a shared space has to know where the people around it are, and
vision alone is a fragile way to find out: it struggles with occlusion and
depends on lighting and weather. The strand I lead uses **ultra-wideband** to
close that gap. UWB ranges by timing very short pulses rather than by measuring signal strength, so
it degrades gracefully where narrowband radio and GNSS do not - it is far less
disturbed by multipath in the reflective urban environments where shared spaces
actually exist, and it does not depend on a clear sightline the way a camera
does. This line of work follows on from our IV 2025 studies.

Two things follow from that, and both are the work:

- **Communicating with the pedestrian, not just detecting them.** UWB is a
  two-way radio link as well as a ranging method, which makes the pedestrian a
  participant in the exchange rather than a passive object being observed.
- **Fusing UWB ranges with AI-based pedestrian detection.** Neither source is
  sufficient alone. Vision gives rich context and classifies what it sees but
  fails under occlusion and poor light; UWB gives a robust range to a device
  under exactly those conditions but knows nothing about the scene. Fused, each
  covers the other's failure mode.

I evaluated three promising methods for pedestrian localization - GNSS,
Bluetooth channel sounding and UWB - in the study below.

### What the measurements showed

We benchmarked the candidate technologies against each other on a Clearpath
Husky A200 carrying two UWB evaluation kits, a Bluetooth 6.0 kit and a GNSS
receiver, with an RTK-corrected GNSS fix as ground truth. Twelve experiments ran
across three environments - open ground, a built-up area, and a roofed area with
limited line of sight to the satellites - measuring the robot-to-pedestrian
distance, including runs where the pedestrian was deliberately occluded.

Distance RMSE, in metres:

| Environment | Smartphone GNSS | Bluetooth 6.0 | UWB |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Open ground | 14.68 | 0.72 | **0.23** |
| Built-up | 17.31 | 1.39 | **0.51** |
| Roofed, limited sky view | 20.76 | 2.01 | **0.63** |

Smartphone-based location, with 15 to 21 metres of error, is useless for deciding
whether a vehicle and a pedestrian are about to occupy the same space. UWB stays inside a metre in every condition tested, and
holds up under the roofed case that degrades GNSS most, which is exactly the
geometry of the covered stops and station approaches this work targets.

## Onboard occupancy detection

Knowing how full each carriage is lets passengers board the right one when the
tram arrives. The approach here uses **CSRNet** - a crowd-counting architecture
that regresses a
density map rather than detecting individual people, which is what makes it
workable in a packed compartment where bodies occlude one another and
person-by-person detection degrades fastest.

My role on this strand is **technical coordinator** and systems expert:

- Coordinating the **data collection** campaigns that produce footage
  representative enough to train against.
- Running the **labelling** effort that turns that footage into usable training
  data.
- Ensuring **GDPR compliance** throughout - an unavoidable constraint when the
  training data is video of identifiable passengers in a public space, and one
  that shapes what can be collected and retained before any model design
  question is reached.
- Evaluating **Jetson platforms** for deployment, since the model has to run on
  the vehicle within a fixed power and thermal budget rather than in a data
  centre.

Running AI models on real-world systems means overcoming significant regulatory
and hardware constraints, not just technical ones. What can lawfully be recorded
determines the training set, and what fits on the embedded platform determines
which model is deployable at all - and those are as much a part of my work as the
development itself.

## Coordination

Beyond the research, the role is substantially about alignment: OptiPEX spans
research groups, industry stakeholders and public transport innovation
initiatives, and technical coordination means keeping those interests pointed at
the same deliverable.
