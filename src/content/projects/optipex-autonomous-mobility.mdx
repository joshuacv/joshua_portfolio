## Context

Autonomous vehicles are comparatively well understood on structured roads. They
are much less well understood in **shared spaces** — the places where vehicles,
cyclists and pedestrians occupy the same ground with no lane markings arbitrating
between them, and where behaviour is negotiated rather than rule-following.

That is where my current work sits: at JKU's Intelligent Transport Systems
department, leading development of last-mile delivery and autonomous mobility
capabilities, and serving as technical lead and coordinator for the EU-funded
**OptiPEX** project.

## Pedestrian risk in shared spaces

We ran autonomous vehicle experiments on a fixed track to evaluate how pedestrians
actually behave and what risks emerge when they share space with an autonomous
vehicle. Running this as a controlled physical experiment rather than a simulation
study was the point — pedestrian behaviour around vehicles is exactly the kind of
thing that simulated models get subtly and consequentially wrong.

Published as *Evaluating pedestrian risks in shared spaces through autonomous
vehicle experiments on a fixed track* at the **36th IEEE Intelligent Vehicles
Symposium (IV 2025)**.

## V2P collision warnings for distracted pedestrians

A companion study compared vehicle-to-pedestrian collision warning strategies
against traditional audio alerts, specifically for **distracted** pedestrians —
the population that conventional alerting is worst at reaching.

Published as *V2P collision warnings for distracted pedestrians: A comparative
study with traditional audio alerts* at **IV 2025**.

## Coordination and teaching

Beyond the research, the role is substantially about alignment: OptiPEX spans
research groups, industry stakeholders and public transport innovation
initiatives, and technical coordination means keeping those interests pointed at
the same deliverable.

I also lecture **"Introduction to Autonomous Vehicles"** at JKU, mentoring
graduate students through autonomy stacks and system integration — which is, in
practice, the fastest way to find out which parts of a stack you actually
understand.

## Where this is heading

The direction I am pushing this work is toward **learning-based methods for
interaction-heavy autonomy**. Shared spaces are the setting where hand-specified
rules degrade fastest, because the behaviour being modelled is social rather than
physical. Predicting and responding to human intent is a learning problem, and
this is the domain where I want to bring that in.
