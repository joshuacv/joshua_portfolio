## The problem

Industrial mobile robots have to work in environments that were never designed
for them. Warehouse and production floors change shape daily: pallets move,
racking gets reconfigured, people and forklifts cut across paths. A robot that
localizes beautifully in a static map degrades badly once the map stops matching
the world.

Fastbots was DS Automotion's platform for exactly that setting — and it had to be
a product, not a demo. That meant every design decision was constrained by what
could be manufactured, serviced by field technicians, and certified for
industrial deployment.

## My role

I was responsible for the **core localization interface** and a set of C++
drivers and control modules covering actuation and battery systems. Alongside
the software work I drove hardware integration and testing across the platform's
first three iterations — the phase where the architecture is still moving and
each cycle has to feed validation results back into the next design.

That put me between two worlds: close enough to the hardware to debug a CAN bus
or an actuator timing issue, and close enough to the architecture to argue about
how the localization layer should be abstracted so it could survive the next
three sensor configurations.

## Approach

The localization interface was designed as an abstraction boundary rather than a
single algorithm. Production robots outlive their sensor choices, so the goal was
that swapping or fusing an estimator did not ripple into the navigation stack
above it.

Working across mechanical, electrical and product teams was not a side activity
here — it was the job. Moving a prototype onto a deployable platform is mostly a
sequence of negotiations about what the software can absorb versus what the
hardware has to fix.

## Outcome

Fastbots shipped as an [award-winning product](https://www.ssi-schaefer.com/de-at/newsroom/news/fastbots-solution-nominiert-1991536), with the localization interface and
control modules I built in the core of the system. The three-iteration cycle I
supported established the validation loop the platform continued to develop on.
